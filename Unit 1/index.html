💡 What is the AI Project Cycle?

The AI Project Cycle is a structured process used to develop an AI-based solution. It helps us move from identifying a problem to creating, testing and deploying an AI solution.

🎯 1. Problem Scoping

Problem scoping means clearly understanding the problem that we want AI to solve.

WHY?

Why does this problem need to be solved?

WHAT?

What exactly is the problem?

WHERE?

Where does the problem occur?

WHO?

Who is affected by the problem?

🌟 Example: Suppose a school wants to identify students who may need additional academic support.

Why? To provide help early.
What? Identify students needing support.
Where? In the school.
Who? Students and teachers.

📥 2. Data Acquisition

Data acquisition means collecting the data required for an AI system.

📊 Example: A weather prediction system may collect temperature, humidity, rainfall and wind-speed data.

🔍 3. Data Exploration

In this stage, the collected data is examined to understand patterns, relationships, missing values and unusual information.

🔎 Example: We may create charts to understand how temperature changes during different months.

🧠 4. Modelling

Modelling is the stage where an AI or machine-learning model learns patterns from the data.

🤖 Example: A model can learn from pictures of different fruits and later predict the type of a new fruit.

📊 5. Evaluation

Evaluation checks how well the AI model performs. We compare its predictions with the expected results.

🎯 Example: If an AI correctly identifies 90 out of 100 test images, its accuracy is 90%.

🚀 6. Deployment

Deployment means making the trained AI system available for actual use.

🌍 Example: After testing a chatbot, it can be deployed on a school's website for students to use.

Background

Imagine an AI system designed to identify possible signs of mental health concerns using available information such as digital or social-media data.

❗ What problem could occur?

The AI system may produce inaccurate or unfair predictions about some people.

🔍 Why might the problem happen?

Bias in Data

Training data may not represent different groups equally.

Overemphasis on Social Media

Social-media information may not provide a complete picture of a person's situation.

Ignoring Social Factors

Important real-world circumstances may not be represented in the data.
